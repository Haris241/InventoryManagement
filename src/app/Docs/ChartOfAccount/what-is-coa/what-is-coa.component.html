What Is a Chart of Accounts?

The Chart of Accounts (COA) is the master list of every account your business uses to record money moving in, out, and around it. Every invoice, payment, and journal entry in Harvora ERP ultimately posts to one of these accounts.

folder_open Group Accounts & Ledger Accounts

Every account in Harvora ERP is one of two kinds. Understanding the difference is the key to reading your Chart of Accounts.

folder

Group Account

A folder that organizes related accounts underneath it, like "Current Assets" or "Current Liabilities." Groups summarize their children on reports but never receive transactions directly.

Not Postable
receipt_long

Ledger Account

The actual "leaf" account that transactions post to — like "Cash in Hand" or "Sales Revenue." Every voucher and journal entry lands on a Ledger account, never a Group.

Postable

category The Five Account Categories

Every account belongs to exactly one category, and each category has a natural "normal balance" — the side (debit or credit) where it usually grows.

Category Normal Balance Grows With Example
Asset Debit Debit Cash, Inventory, Equipment
Liability Credit Credit Loans Payable, Accounts Payable
Equity Credit Credit Owner's Capital, Retained Earnings
Revenue Credit Credit Sales, Service Income
Expense Debit Debit Rent, Salaries, Utilities

In practice: you rarely need to think about debit and credit directly — Harvora applies the correct side automatically based on the account's category whenever you record a transaction.

label Account Usage Tags

Some Ledger accounts play a special role in the system — Harvora uses these tags to know which accounts to offer when you're recording a cash sale, a bank transfer, or a customer invoice.

None Cash Bank Customer Supplier Tax

Cash and Bank accounts must be Asset accounts. Customer and Supplier accounts must be Asset or Liability accounts. Group accounts never carry a usage tag.

tag Account Numbering

Account codes are generated automatically, grouped by category into predictable number ranges so related accounts always sit near each other.

Range Category

200000 series Liabilities
300000 series Equity
400000 series Revenue

rocket_launch You Don't Start From Scratch

When you register your business on Harvora, a starter Chart of Accounts is created for you automatically — the five root categories plus a few essential accounts every business needs. You build on top of this rather than starting from a blank sheet.

Account Code Kind Why It's There
Assets 100000 Group Root category for everything your business owns.
Liabilities 200000 Group Root category for everything your business owes.
Equity 300000 Group Root category for the owner's stake in the business.
Revenue 400000 Group Root category for income earned.
Expenses 500000 Group Root category for costs incurred.
Current Assets 100001 Group Groups assets you'll use or convert to cash within a year.
Non-Current Assets 100002 Group Groups longer-term assets like equipment or property.
Current Liabilities 200001 Group Groups obligations due within a year.
Non-Current Liabilities 200002 Group Groups longer-term obligations like loans.
Opening Balance Equity 300001 Ledger Balances out the opening figures you enter when you first set up an account.
Retained Earnings 300002 Ledger Carries forward your accumulated profit or loss year over year.

These starter accounts are protected — you can't rename or delete them — since your reports depend on them always being present. Everything else you add sits alongside or underneath them.
