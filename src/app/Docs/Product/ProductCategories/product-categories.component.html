What Are Product Categories?

Product categories organize your catalogue into a hierarchy — so instead of scrolling through thousands of individual items, you can browse, report, and even post accounting entries at whatever level makes sense: broad group or specific leaf.

lightbulb Why Product Categories Matter

As a product catalogue grows, flat lists stop working — finding, reporting on, and pricing items becomes slow and error-prone. Categories solve this by grouping related products under a shared structure, the same way folders organize files. A well-built category tree makes browsing faster, keeps reporting meaningful, and lets you apply rules — like which accounts a sale or purchase should post to — at a group level instead of item by item.

account_tree A Hierarchical Structure

Categories in Harvora ERP nest under one another to any depth — "Garments" can contain "Menswear," which can contain "Shirts." Every category is one of two kinds, and Harvora tracks which one automatically as your tree changes.

folder

Group Category

Has at least one child category underneath it. Once a category gets its first child, it automatically becomes a group and can no longer be deactivated or moved directly.

label

Leaf Category

Has no children — the end of a branch. Only leaf categories can be activated, deactivated, or moved to a new parent.

badge Name & Code

  • Every category needs a Name and a Code — both required.
  • Codes must be unique across your entire catalogue, not just within a parent.
  • Codes are stored in uppercase, so fab-01 and FAB-01 are treated as the same code.

rule Moving & Deactivating Categories

Because reports and account rules can depend on a category's position in the tree, changing that structure is restricted to leaf categories only.

  • A category with children can't be moved to a new parent — only leaf categories can be moved.
  • A category with children can't be deactivated — only leaf categories can be activated or deactivated.
  • Activating a category requires its parent to already be active.
  • An active category can never be moved under an inactive parent.
  • A category can never be set as its own parent.

link Attaching Chart of Accounts

By default, GRNs and sales invoices post to the accounts set in your global inventory settings. If you want certain categories to post to their own accounts instead — useful once you're tracking inventory value or cost of goods sold category by category — you can attach up to three accounts to any category. This is entirely optional.

inventory_2

Inventory Account

Must be an Asset account. Used when goods in this category are received.

sell

Sales Account

Must be a Revenue account. Used when goods in this category are sold.

Cost of Goods Sold Account

Must be an Expense account. Used to record the cost side of a sale.

low_priority How Harvora Picks the Account

When a GRN or sales invoice is posted, Harvora looks for an account in this order and uses the first one it finds:

Priority Source When It Applies
1 Leaf Category The product's own category has this account attached.
2 Parent Category The leaf category doesn't have it, but a parent up the tree does.
3 Global Settings No category in the chain has it — the system-wide default account is used.

This means you only need to attach accounts where you actually want category-specific posting — everything else falls back to your global defaults with no extra setup.
